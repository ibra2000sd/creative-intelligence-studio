Daylight Ledger

A household's energy told as one day: sunrise, noon, dusk, night. Warm paper grounds, a serif with some life in it, and a single arc that the sun travels in every deliverable.

Why it fits
Homeowners think about energy in terms of their day, not in kilowatts. A warm editorial language separates Haldora from both the industrial and the gadget ends of the category, and the day arc gives the logo, the scroll story, the chart, the app dial and the product light one shared idea.
Feels
Calm confidence: the feeling of a house that is taken care of.
Type
Fraunces / Instrument Sans: A soft, slightly old-style serif against a plain modern sans
Motion
balanced: Unhurried, like light moving across a room
Imagery
Flat, layered landscape horizons with paper grain; no photography of people or homes (arc, horizon, grain)
Signature
The day arc: a sun on a dashed arc above a horizon, positioned by the time of day.
Usability
Warm low-chroma grounds keep long reading comfortable. Time-of-day colour shifts must never carry meaning on their own; labels do.
Needs
Scroll-linked timeline on the marketing page Light and dark schemes mapped to day and night Arabic typeface with matching weight range
Risks
A serif display face can read as traditional; needs the indigo and the arc geometry to stay modern. Amber on paper fails text contrast, so amber is reserved for fills and marks.

The battery as a precision instrument. Graphite panels, a phosphor signal colour, monospaced numerals and measured grids: every surface looks like a readout.

Why it fits
Speaks to the technically curious buyer and makes the software feel exact. It is the convention of the category, which makes it instantly legible as energy technology but hard to tell apart from it.
Feels
Control and precision.
Type
JetBrains Mono / Instrument Sans: Monospaced headings, tabular numerals everywhere
Motion
subtle: Instant and mechanical, like a gauge settling
Imagery
Technical line drawings and measured grids (grid, contour)
Signature
A live tick ruler along the edge of every surface.
Usability
Dense information suits the portal but intimidates first-time visitors. Dark default raises glare problems outdoors on phones.
Needs
Tabular numeral font features Dark-first theming
Risks
Looks like every other energy dashboard: the brief asks to avoid green-on-black. Cold tone works against the 'belongs in a home' goal.

Energy as something you never have to think about. Cool, pale surfaces, one saturated cobalt, a geometric sans and a great deal of empty space; the product is shown large and alone.

Why it fits
Positions the Cell as a calm household appliance. Strong for product photography and for trust, weaker at explaining what the battery actually does through a day.
Feels
Relief: nothing here needs your attention.
Type
Instrument Sans / Instrument Sans: One geometric sans in two weights
Motion
subtle: Slow fades and a gentle drift
Imagery
Large product renders on seamless pale grounds (orbit, bloom)
Signature
A soft ring that pulses once when power changes direction.
Usability
Very low visual noise helps scanning. Pale-on-pale surfaces need careful borders for low-vision users.
Needs
High-quality product renders for every viewport
Risks
Close to gadget minimalism, which the brief asks to avoid. Depends on product renders the client does not have yet.
